AI 越来越有用，新增推理算力就一定赚钱吗？

发布于 2026 年 10 月 30 日；资料截止 2026 年 10 月 28 日。

10 月 27 日，一条关于 GB300 的推文把推理算力争论变得具体：dax 估计，OpenRouter 前一天的 DeepSeek 流量，几乎用一柜 GB300 就能服务。原推说的是整柜，不是单张 GPU；GB300 NVL72包含 72 个 GPU 和 36 个 Grace CPU。

这个说法没有得到生产容量验证。公开流量与性能测试的日期、模型、缓存比例并未对齐，更缺少高峰、尾延迟和容灾要求。vLLM 10 月 23 日的测试确实展示了很高吞吐，但统计包含缓存输入、未缓存输入与输出，不能当成纯生成性能。OpenRouter 的公开数据也不覆盖所有私有流量，更不是全球市场。

作者随后澄清，质疑的是开放模型推理需求，同时承认大玩家吸收大量算力。由此宣布“全球算力已经过剩”太早。但它提出了更值得投资者追问的问题：AI 可以越来越有用，2027—2028 年交付的新增推理容量，却未必获得足以覆盖资本成本的收入。

Token、任务、付款、回报，是四件事

Token 不是统一的计算单位。一百万缓存输入、一百万新输入和一百万输出，资源消耗不同；模型架构、批量和延迟要求也会改变成本。

更有用的关系是：

推理计算需求 ≈ 完成任务数 × 每任务所需计算量。

比较时必须固定任务质量与服务标准，每个成功任务的计算量也要包含相关失败尝试和重试。Agent 可以增加调用、搜索和验证，也可以通过缓存、小模型与更好的工作流降低每次成功任务的成本。因此，token 暴增不必然对应 GPU-hours 同比例增长。

付费需求又是下一层：用户愿意为完成什么任务付多少钱？最后才是资本回报：收入扣除运营成本后，是否足以回收设备投入并补偿资金成本？机器被租走，不等于终端客户创造了足够利润；设备满载，也可能只是价格降得足够低。

对新增采购，还要考虑存量容量、退役替换、地域和冗余。训练与后训练确实需要算力，但不能用它们替纯推理投资补上论证缺口。

悲观论最强的证据，不是“AI 没人用”

首先，同等能力正在变便宜。Epoch AI 2025 年 3 月的研究发现，达到不同能力门槛的最低 API 价格下降很快。但这是历史价格研究，包含竞争与利润率变化，不能直接当成未来物理成本降幅。

其次，降价不保证需求无限反弹。2025 年 12 月的 NBER 工作论文使用 OpenRouter 与 Azure 数据，估计初步的短期价格弹性略高于一。这支持一定反弹，却不足以证明新需求必然吞下所有效率红利；它也不是长期 GPU 用量或用电弹性的结论。

局部宽松已有更近的信号。Silicon Data 10 月 21 日的分析显示，10 月 8—16 日，Nebius 单一欧洲区域的 H100 spot 报价多数时间贴近每 GPU-hour 0.79 美元的下限，同期 B300 表现不同。关键限制是：这是九天的可中断资源报价，不是成交租价或利用率。它说明市场分化，不能证明全球闲置。

所以，悲观论最扎实的版本是：效率与竞争可能同时降低单位任务资源消耗、削弱定价权。即便 AI 使用量继续增长，高成本项目仍可能无法回本。

乐观论也有真实证据，但日期很重要

容量约束不全是采购宣传。Anthropic 2026 年 4 月 20 日承认增长影响可靠性与高峰性能；5 月 6 日扩容公告随后提高 Claude Code 额度，并取消此前高峰时段的额度下调。这是需求曾经压到产品体验的证据，不能写成十月底那些限制仍在持续。

Google 5 月 19 日披露，跨产品月处理量超过 3.2 quadrillion tokens，约为上一年的七倍。真实处理量增长很重要，但混合模型、模态与缓存后，不能直接换算设备需求。微软 4 月财报也同时报告推理吞吐改善与广泛客户需求超过供给；后者并非纯推理口径。

乐观论最强的版本是：更便宜的智能解锁新用途，agent 增加任务密度，用户要求更高质量和更低延迟，可能足以吸收降本带来的容量。问题在于，过去发生过紧张，并不能证明两年后每个新增项目都能赚钱。

红杉 David Cahn 在 2024 年的讨论抓住了这个区别：计算降价可以有利于创新，却伤害基础设施投资者。值得保留的是这个逻辑，而不是把当年的收入缺口数字搬到今天。

2027—2028 年：四种条件情景

以下是条件情景，不是预测。举例说，若可用硬件规模增至三倍，平均单位硬件效率增至四倍，同等任务承载能力就是十二倍；若需求只增至五倍，利用率便承压。这里的数字纯属演示，效率改善必须统一核算，不能重复计入硬件换代。

条件情景 经济结果
新任务与付费需求增长足以吸收供给 容量扩张有支撑，但利润率和股票估值仍需单独检验
使用量增长，效率提升更快 红利更多流向客户，硬件与服务支出增长可能低于预期
新硬件成本优势推动换代 新机销售强，旧 GPU 租价与残值却承压
付费增长落后于预订容量交付 降价与低利用率可能引发延期采购、缩减资本开支和减值

软件与采用者也不自动获胜：降本可能被竞争转成产品降价，AI 还可能侵蚀原有席位收入。

股票不必等到机器闲置才反应

对 NVIDIA、AMD 等供应商，压力可能先体现在下一轮订单和交付节奏，再传到产品组合与毛利。总计算量增长也不等于某家收入增长，替代芯片和自研方案会影响份额。

GPU 云与租赁商更直接面对租价、利用率下降和相对固定的融资成本。大型平台则有两面：采购放缓、推理降本有利于现金流，但旧资产仍需折旧，云收入也可能受压。HBM、网络、服务器和电力链的影响，要看具体建设计划与合同。

股票交易的是相对于预期的盈利，而非“AI 是否还有用”。即使利润继续增长，只要低于原先预期，盈利下修与估值倍数压缩仍可能同时发生；大型科技股的变化还会通过指数权重与融资条件传导。没有必要据此猜一个大盘跌幅。

接下来，观察什么？

比总 token 曲线更有用的是五组指标：

  1. 可比租价与出租率：同型号、地区和服务保障下的实际成交、续约与利用率，而非最低 spot 报价。
  2. 每个成功任务的经济性：固定质量和延迟，跟踪成本、任务量及客户愿付价格。
  3. 终端付费与服务商毛利：跟踪最终客户为 AI 产品和服务支付的金额，区分模型公司支出、云合同与融资，避免把同一笔需求在产业链各层重复计算。
  4. 旧资产与新订单：残值、经济寿命、新机溢价，以及采购预算、延期和取消条款。
  5. 信号是否同时恶化：降价本身可能是好事；若利用率下降、收入增长不足和未来采购下调一起出现，过建论才明显增强。

真正需要证明的，不是 AI 会不会继续被使用，而是新增推理容量能否以足够的价格被使用，并产生足以支撑资本投入与盈利预期的回报。技术成功与投资成功之间，仍隔着一张现金流表。

More Useful AI Does Not Guarantee Profitable Inference Capacity

Published October 30, 2026. Research cutoff: October 28, 2026.

On October 27, a post about GB300 made the inference-capacity debate concrete. Dax estimated that almost all of OpenRouter’s DeepSeek traffic from the previous day could be served by one GB300 rack. The original claim was about a rack, not a single GPU: a GB300 NVL72 contains 72 GPUs and 36 Grace CPUs.

That claim has not been validated as production sizing. The public traffic figures and performance benchmark were not aligned by date, model, or cache mix, let alone peak load, tail latency, and failover requirements. The October 23 vLLM benchmark does demonstrate high throughput, but it counts cached input, uncached input, and output tokens—not just newly generated output. OpenRouter’s public data also excludes some private traffic and is not a census of the global market.

In a follow-up, the author narrowed the criticism to open-model inference demand while acknowledging that large buyers absorb substantial capacity. Declaring a global compute glut from this evidence would be premature. But the debate raises a more important investment question: AI can become increasingly useful while new inference capacity delivered in 2027–2028 fails to earn its cost of capital.

Tokens, tasks, payments, and returns are different things

A token is not a standardized unit of computation. A million cached input tokens, a million fresh input tokens, and a million output tokens impose different costs. Architecture, batching, and latency requirements change those costs further.

A more useful relationship is:

Inference compute demand ≈ completed tasks × compute required per task.

Comparisons need consistent task quality and service requirements, with compute per successful task including associated failed attempts and retries. Agents can add calls, search, and verification. They can also reduce the cost of a successful task through caching, smaller models, and better workflows. Explosive token growth therefore need not produce proportional growth in GPU-hours.

Paying demand is another step: what will customers pay to accomplish those tasks? Capital returns come after that. Does revenue, after operating costs, repay the equipment investment and compensate investors for the cost of funding it? Rented machines do not establish profitable end-user demand. Even fully utilized machines may simply be priced low enough to stay busy.

New purchases also depend on existing capacity, retirements, replacement, geography, and redundancy. Training and post-training need compute, but they cannot fill a hole in an investment case specifically about inference.

The strongest bearish case is not “nobody uses AI”

First, a given level of capability is getting cheaper. Epoch AI’s March 2025 research found rapid declines in the lowest API prices meeting different capability thresholds. But these are historical prices, influenced by competition and margins—not a direct forecast of future physical computing costs.

Second, lower prices do not guarantee unlimited demand rebound. A December 2025 NBER working paper, using OpenRouter and Azure data, estimated preliminary short-run price elasticities just above one. That supports some rebound, but not the assumption that new demand must absorb every efficiency gain. It is not an estimate of long-run GPU consumption or electricity elasticity, either.

There is also more recent evidence of localized softness. An October 21 analysis by Silicon Data found that, during October 8–16, Nebius H100 spot offers in one European region spent most of their time near a floor of $0.79 per GPU-hour. B300 pricing behaved differently in the same window. The crucial qualification: these were nine days of offered prices for interruptible capacity, not realized rental rates or utilization. They demonstrate segmentation, not global idleness.

The strongest bearish argument is therefore that efficiency and competition can reduce both resource consumption per task and pricing power. AI usage may keep growing while high-cost infrastructure projects fail to pay back their investment.

The bullish evidence is real—but its dates matter

Capacity constraints are not merely procurement rhetoric. On April 20, 2026, Anthropic acknowledged that growth had affected reliability and peak-time performance. Its May 6 capacity announcement subsequently raised Claude Code limits and removed the previous peak-hour limit reduction. That is evidence that demand had strained the product. It is not evidence that those particular restrictions persisted into late October.

On May 19, Google reported processing more than 3.2 quadrillion tokens per month across its products, roughly seven times the prior-year level. Growth in actual processing matters, but mixed models, modalities, and caching prevent a direct conversion into hardware demand. Microsoft’s April earnings report likewise described both improved inference throughput and broad customer demand exceeding supply. The latter was not an inference-only measure.

The strongest bullish argument is that cheaper intelligence unlocks new uses, agents increase task intensity, and customers demand better results at lower latency. Together, these forces could absorb the capacity released by efficiency improvements. What they have not established is that every project arriving two years later will earn an adequate return.

David Cahn’s 2024 Sequoia essay captured the distinction: cheaper computing can benefit innovation while hurting infrastructure investors. The useful takeaway is that logic, not transplanting a 2024 revenue-gap estimate into the present.

Four conditional scenarios for 2027–2028

These are conditional scenarios, not forecasts. For illustration, suppose available hardware triples and average efficiency per unit of hardware quadruples. Capacity for equivalent tasks would rise twelvefold. If demand rises only fivefold, utilization comes under pressure. Those numbers are hypothetical, and efficiency must be measured consistently so hardware-generation gains are not counted twice.

Conditional scenario Economic implication
New tasks and paying demand absorb the additional supply Expansion has support, but margins and equity valuations still need separate justification
Usage grows, but efficiency improves faster More of the benefit reaches customers; hardware and service spending may grow less than expected
Superior economics drive hardware replacement New equipment can sell well while older GPU rental rates and residual values weaken
Paying demand lags the delivery of booked capacity Lower prices and utilization may lead to deferred purchases, capital-spending cuts, and impairments

Software vendors and AI adopters are not automatic winners. Competition may pass cost savings through as lower product prices, while AI can also erode existing seat-based revenue.

Stocks need not wait for idle machines

For suppliers such as NVIDIA and AMD, pressure could first appear in expectations for the next order cycle and delivery schedules, then flow into product mix and margins. Growth in total computation is not the same as revenue growth for an individual supplier: alternative chips and in-house designs affect market share.

GPU clouds and rental operators face a more direct mismatch between falling rental rates or utilization and relatively fixed financing costs. Large platforms have a mixed exposure. Slower purchasing and cheaper inference can help cash flow, but existing assets still incur depreciation, and cloud revenue may face pricing pressure. The consequences for HBM, networking, servers, and power infrastructure depend on specific construction plans and contracts.

Equities trade on earnings relative to expectations, not on whether AI remains useful. Profits can keep growing while disappointing prior expectations, allowing earnings revisions and valuation-multiple compression to occur together. Changes in large technology companies can then transmit through index weights and financing conditions. None of this establishes a particular downside target for the broader market.

What to monitor next

Five sets of indicators are more useful than the aggregate token curve:

  1. Comparable rental rates and utilization. Track realized transactions, renewals, and occupancy for the same hardware, region, and service guarantees—not the cheapest spot offer.
  2. Economics per successful task. Hold quality and latency requirements constant, then track cost, task volume, and willingness to pay.
  3. End-customer spending and provider gross margins. Track what final customers pay for AI products and services. Distinguish that from model-provider spending, cloud contracts, and financing to avoid counting the same demand at multiple layers of the supply chain.
  4. Old assets and new orders. Watch residual values, economic lifetimes, new-hardware premiums, purchasing budgets, and deferral or cancellation terms.
  5. Whether the signals deteriorate together. Falling prices alone can be good news. The overbuilding case becomes materially stronger when utilization falls, revenue growth disappoints, and future purchasing is revised down at the same time.

The proposition that needs proving is not that AI will continue to be used. It is that new inference capacity will be used at prices high enough to justify the capital invested and the earnings expected from it. Between technological success and investment success sits a cash-flow statement.
